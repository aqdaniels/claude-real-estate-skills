# Zones, grading, and hidden risks

Six zones cover the whole house. Grade each from evidence, list what the evidence can't confirm, then price. The point of zoning is to make omissions visible: a report with no line items under Plumbing should say "no plumbing visible in photos — unconfirmed", not silently price it at zero.

## Grades

| Grade | Meaning | Pricing rule |
|---|---|---|
| A | Sound, recent, no work needed | $0 except cosmetic touch-up if any |
| B | Serviceable, dated or worn, works | Repair/refresh in rent-ready; replace in mid-grade if comps show it |
| C | Needs work now; partial failure or clearly end-of-life | Repair or replace in all scenarios |
| D | Replace, or unknown in a house old enough that failure is likely | Replace in worst case; repair in rent-ready if evidence allows; call out the assumption |

When photos don't show a zone at all, grade it "U" (unconfirmed) and treat as B for rent-ready, C for mid-grade, D for worst case — stated in the Assumptions section.

## Zone 1 — Envelope (roof, exterior walls, windows, doors, gutters, drainage)

Look for: roof material and visible age (curling, missing tabs, moss, patching, multiple layers), flashing at penetrations, gutters and downspouts, fascia/soffit rot, siding type and condition, paint peel, window type (single-pane wood, aluminum slider, vinyl), broken seals (fog between panes), door condition, grading toward the house, standing water.

Can't confirm from photos: roof decking, underlayment, remaining life (need age from permit or disclosure), attic ventilation, window operation.

Typical line items: roof replace or repair (by roof sqft ≈ footprint × 1.15–1.4 for pitch), gutter replace (linear ft), fascia/soffit repair, siding repair or replace (sqft), exterior paint (sqft wall), window replace (each), exterior doors (each), grading/drainage correction.

## Zone 2 — Mechanical (electrical service, panel, wiring, HVAC, water heater)

Look for: panel brand and age (Federal Pacific, Zinsco, Challenger, Sylvania = replace), breaker vs fuse, visible wiring type at panel or attic (cloth, knob-and-tube, aluminum = flag), outlet style (two-prong = likely ungrounded), GFCI presence in kitchen/bath, HVAC condenser age from data plate if visible, furnace/air handler type, ductwork visible in attic, water heater age and type, venting.

Can't confirm: wiring type inside walls, panel capacity vs load, HVAC function and refrigerant type (R-22 units are end-of-life), duct leakage.

Typical line items: panel replace, full rewire (by sqft, only when evidence or era demands), add circuits/GFCIs, HVAC full system replace (by tonnage ≈ 1 ton per 500–600 sqft in the South), condenser only, ductwork replace, water heater replace (tank or tankless), mini-splits (each).

## Zone 3 — Plumbing (supply, drain, fixtures, water heater location, sewer)

Look for: visible supply pipe material under sinks and at water heater (galvanized, copper, PEX, polybutylene grey pipe = flag), drain material (cast iron, PVC), staining and soft spots at ceilings below baths, mold at bases, fixture age, water pressure complaints in disclosures, tree roots near sewer path.

Can't confirm: sewer line condition (needs camera scope), supply lines inside walls, slab leaks, water heater expansion tank/TPR compliance.

Typical line items: repipe supply (by fixture count or sqft), replace drain stack, sewer line repair or replace (linear ft; trenchless vs dig), fixture replacements (each), water heater relocate, leak remediation and drywall repair.

## Zone 4 — Interior surfaces (walls, ceilings, flooring, trim, interior doors, paint)

Look for: flooring type and wear, carpet age, subfloor softness signs (uneven flooring, gaps), water stains on ceilings, cracked drywall/plaster, popcorn ceilings (asbestos risk pre-1980), trim damage, door operation, paint condition, smoke/pet odor mentions.

Can't confirm: subfloor condition under carpet, plaster keying, lead paint (pre-1978 assume present).

Typical line items: interior paint (by sqft floor × ~3 for wall/ceiling area or by sqft floor as all-in), LVP/laminate flooring (sqft), carpet (sqft), refinish hardwood (sqft), drywall repair (sqft) or full replace, popcorn removal/skim (sqft ceiling), trim and doors (each), subfloor repair (sqft).

## Zone 5 — Kitchen and baths (cabinets, counters, appliances, tile, tubs/showers, vanities, toilets, ventilation)

Look for: cabinet material and condition (doors, hinges, water damage under sink), counter material, appliance age, backsplash, tile condition and grout, tub/shower surround, vanity, toilet age (pre-1994 = high-flow, replace in mid-grade), exhaust fans present, layout functionality.

Can't confirm: water damage inside cabinet boxes, tile substrate, shower pan integrity.

Typical line items: kitchen at three spec levels (rental / mid / high), bath at three spec levels, cabinet reface vs replace, counters (linear ft or sqft), appliance package, tile (sqft), tub reglaze vs replace, vanity and toilet (each), exhaust fans.

## Zone 6 — Structure and site (foundation, framing, crawlspace/slab, pests, garage, hardscape, landscaping, fencing)

Look for: foundation type (slab, pier and beam, crawlspace, basement), stair-step cracks in masonry, diagonal cracks at door/window corners, doors that don't close, sloping floors, piers visibly leaning, skirting damage, termite tubes or mud tunnels, wood rot at sill, standing water at perimeter, tree roots against foundation, fence condition, driveway cracking, detached structures, overgrowth.

Can't confirm: settlement extent (need level survey), termite damage inside framing, pier condition without crawl, slab cracks under flooring.

Typical line items: pier/underpinning (each), leveling (by sqft or job), sill/joist repair, termite treatment and damage repair, crawlspace moisture barrier/encapsulation, drainage (French drain linear ft), fence (linear ft), driveway/walk (sqft), tree removal (each), landscaping clean-up (job), demolition/haul-off (per dumpster).

## Hidden risks by era and region

Use year built and region to pre-load risks that photos never show. Each becomes a row in the Hidden Risks table with a trigger, a cost range if it materializes (from cost-tables.md), and a verification step.

| Era / condition | Risk | Verify by |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-1950 | Knob-and-tube or cloth wiring, galvanized supply, cast iron/clay sewer, lead paint, plaster over lath, balloon framing, no insulation | Panel and attic look, sewer scope, lead test, insulation check |
| 1950–1970 | Galvanized supply nearing failure, cast iron drains, asbestos floor tile/siding/duct wrap, ungrounded outlets, undersized service (60–100A) | Under-sink pipe check, scope, asbestos sampling if disturbing |
| 1965–1975 | Aluminum branch wiring (fire risk; insurers may decline) | Look at wire color at panel/outlets; electrician letter |
| 1960–1990 | Federal Pacific / Zinsco / Challenger / Sylvania panels | Panel label |
| 1978–1995 | Polybutylene supply lines (grey pipe) — insurers may decline | Under-sink and water heater look |
| 1980–2000 | Polybutylene, early EIFS stucco, Masonite siding | Siding tap test, stucco probe |
| 2001–2009 (Gulf Coast, esp. post-2005 rebuilds) | Chinese drywall (sulfur, blackened copper) | Look at exposed copper at AC coil/wiring; sulfur odor |
| Any age with mature trees | Sewer root intrusion | Camera scope ($200–500 — always recommend) |
| Pier and beam, South | Termite (Formosan in LA/FL/TX), rot, sagging | Termite letter/contract; crawl inspection |
| Slab, expansive clay (TX, parts of LA) | Foundation movement | Level survey; look at brick cracks and door frames |
| Flood zone A/AE or prior flood | Hidden moisture/mold in walls, corroded electrical, warped subfloor | Moisture meter; flood history disclosure |
| Coastal/hurricane | Roof deck attachment, window rating, insurance requirements (FL 4-point, wind mitigation) | Wind mitigation report; roof permit date |
| Any | Open/expired permits, unpermitted additions | County permit search |
| Any vacant > 6 months | Frozen/burst pipes (cold climates), vandalism, HVAC theft, squatter damage | Walkthrough; utility history |

## Photo triage

When photos are provided: number them, and for each write one line — zone(s) covered, condition observed, grade implication. Do this before pricing so the reader can audit which photo drove which line. If a zone has no photo coverage, say so at the top of that zone. Don't infer a zone from an adjacent one (a nice kitchen says nothing about the panel).

## Inspection report triage

Pull every finding into a table: location, finding, inspector severity (their words), zone, priced line item. Inspectors flag many trivial items; keep them (they are what the buyer will negotiate) but mark them as minor and price them as a single "punch list" line unless individually material.
