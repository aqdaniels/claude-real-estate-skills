# Trade-off analysis: subject vs new build (vs keep leasing), with 1/5/10-year returns

Every report includes this section. It answers what the rest of the report doesn't: *would the same money do better in new construction?* — and for an owner-user, *would the business do better staying a tenant?* — and *what does the position look like at years 1, 5 and 10?*

## Part A — Subject vs new build

Compare the subject (as-is, or as-repositioned if capital is planned) with building the equivalent property new — same type, sqft, and quality tier in the same submarket, on purchased land or on the subject's own land if the honest answer is "tear down and rebuild."

### Research (add to the parallel batch)

- `<city> <type> construction cost per square foot <year>` — retail shell, office shell, tilt-wall/pre-engineered metal warehouse; local GC or appraiser sources over national indexes; say whether hard cost only and whether TI/finish is included
- `<submarket> commercial land for sale` — $/sqft or $/acre, zoned for the use; note site work (fill, drainage, detention — heavy in South Louisiana)
- `<city> new construction <type> lease rate` — the rent premium new gets over 1980s–2000s stock (often 20–40% for retail/industrial, less certain for office)
- Incentives that favor new build or gut rehab: Opportunity Zone, Restoration Tax Abatement (LA, for rehab of existing structures — favors the subject), ITEP, Enterprise Zone, impact-fee waivers, TIF
- Entitlement and construction timeline for the jurisdiction (commercial: 18–30 months entitlement-to-CO is common)

### Comparison table

| | Subject (buy [+ capital/reposition]) | New build |
|---|---|---|
| Land / acquisition | purchase price | land + site work (or $0 on subject's site + demolition) |
| Hard cost | planned capital / TI | $/sqft × GSF |
| Soft cost (A&E, permits, fees, financing, contingency) | ~10–15% of capital | 20–25% of hard cost |
| Leasing cost to stabilize | TI + LC on vacant/rollover space | TI + LC on 100% |

State the verdict in one sentence: which option creates more equity per dollar, and which per month. They are often different answers, and sub-$5M buyers rarely have the pre-leasing to make new build win on time.

## Part A′ — Owner-user: buy vs keep leasing

Replace (or add beside) the new-build column with **keep leasing**:

| | Buy subject | Keep leasing |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cash | down payment + closing + capital + moving/build-out | security deposit / renewal costs |
| Annual occupancy cost yr 1 | debt service + non-recovered opex + reserves − 3rd-party rent | rent + NNN/CAM |
| Occupancy cost growth | mostly fixed (fixed-rate debt); opex at 3% | escalations per lease (2–3% fixed or CPI) + market reset at expiration |
| Tax treatment | depreciation + interest deductible (entity structure matters) | rent deductible |
| Equity build | principal paydown + appreciation | none |
| Flexibility | can sublet/expand/sell; exit takes months | can walk at expiration; landlord can decline renewal |
| Risk | capital items, vacancy of 3rd-party space, balloon/refi | rent shock, relocation, loss of location |

Breakeven year = when cumulative (buy − lease) cash cost is overtaken by equity built.

## Part B — 1 / 5 / 10-year returns and equity

Run a simple annual projection for the subject and each alternative.

### Inputs (show them)

| Input | Default |
|---|---|
| Rent growth | submarket trend; default 2.5%; contractual escalations override for in-place leases |
| Expense growth | 3% |
| Rollover | renewal probability, downtime, TI/LC per `underwriting.md` |
| Exit cap | entry market cap + 25–50 bps; value = forward NOI ÷ exit cap |

| Selling costs at exit | 4–6% (only in the "if sold" line) |

One row block per scenario. Report levered IRR at year 5 and year 10 assuming a sale; unlevered IRR beside it.

### Reading it

- Year 1 is usually thin or negative for value-add and new build; say so.
- The big rollover year (from the lease abstract) should be visible as a dip — if it isn't, the projection is missing leasing costs.
- Year 5 is where paths diverge; year 10 shows whether the older building's second capital cycle (roof, RTUs, parking) erodes the gap.
- Flag the crossover year, if any.

Every projected number is `est`. Keep the section to one comparison table, one returns table, and three or four sentences of interpretation.
